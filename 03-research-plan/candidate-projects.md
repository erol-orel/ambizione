# Choosing the project

You asked me to help choose between candidates. Below is the comparison, a recommendation, and a
sketch of the recommended project. The reasoning matters more than the conclusion — if you
disagree with a premise, say so and the conclusion may change.

## The constraint that decides this

Ambizione is not a project grant with a person attached. It is a **career instrument that tests
whether you are becoming an independent principal investigator.** Two facts about your dossier
therefore dominate the choice:

- Every one of your ~15 papers is with Olivia Keiser's group.
- The Legionella project is formally hers — project leader *and* sponsor on BASEC 2026-00324.

So the question is not "which topic is most interesting". It is **"which topic lets a referee
see a research line that is unmistakably yours?"** A scientifically excellent proposal that reads
as a continuation of your PI's programme scores badly on the criterion the scheme exists to test.

## The candidates

### A. Legionella / environmental infectious disease — *extended well beyond the cantonal study*

**For.** You have something almost nobody has: cantonal case registry (2003–present) linked at
the level of individual **SIG hot water installations**, with technical parameters, plus
MeteoSwiss and SITG covariates — and ethics already granted. Feasibility is evidenced rather
than promised, which is the hardest thing to demonstrate in a proposal. You have already
convinced three funders (SIG, OCEN, Service du Médecin Cantonal) to commit money, which is
itself PI-shaped evidence. And the topic sits on a live Swiss policy tension.

**Against.** The cantonal project is Keiser's. Proposed as-is, it makes the independence problem
worse, not better. It only works if the Ambizione is visibly *bigger and different* — and the
cantonal study is repositioned as your preliminary data.

### B. LiteRev / NLP for evidence synthesis

**For.** First-author JMIR paper, a working tool, and a demonstrated capacity to build software.

**Against.** The field was overrun by LLM-based systematic review tooling after 2023. Competing
now against well-resourced groups and commercial products on a methods-service contribution, in
a Division III panel that will ask what the *biomedical* question is. This is your weakest option
on originality and topicality, and topicality is an explicit criterion.

### C. HIV / ML prediction in sub-Saharan Africa

**For.** Where your first-author PLoS ONE paper and PhD sit; the record supports it directly.

**Against.** It *is* Keiser's programme. On the criterion that decides Ambizione, this is the
worst of the three. It also leans on data and partnerships that came through her network.

## Recommendation

**Candidate A, restructured** — and the restructuring is the whole point.

Do not propose the cantonal study at four-year scale. Propose the question the cantonal study
made you able to ask, and let the cantonal study serve as the pilot that proves you can answer it.

### The framing I would build on

> Switzerland is lowering domestic hot water temperatures to meet energy targets — heat pumps,
> solar thermal, reduced setpoints, low-temperature distribution. *Legionella* proliferation is
> temperature-dependent. Every degree saved is energy gained and, plausibly, risk added. **Nobody
> has quantified that trade-off with linked real-world data at population scale**, so the current
> normative guidance rests on microbiological plausibility rather than on measured population
> risk — and building operators, cantons and the Confederation are making irreversible
> infrastructure decisions in that gap, right now.

Why this framing is strong for this scheme specifically:

- **Original**, and the originality is structural rather than incremental — it is a question no
  one can currently answer, not a method applied to a new dataset.
- **Topical** in the most literal sense: the Energy Strategy 2050 building retrofit programme is
  running now, and the 2022–23 energy crisis produced a real natural experiment in setpoint
  reduction across the country.
- **Feasible**, evidenced by the Geneva pilot and the ethics approval already in hand.
- **Impactful** in a way you can name concretely: it produces the evidence base for a norm
  (SIA 385 and cantonal energy regulation), and the counterfactual — decisions made anyway,
  without evidence — is easy for a referee to feel.
- **Yours.** It is not global-health HIV modelling. It sits at the intersection of environmental
  epidemiology, building physics, econometrics and health economics — a combination that follows
  from *your* biography and not from your PI's.

### Sketch — four work packages

**WP1 — From Geneva to Switzerland: a linkage framework.**
Generalise the case↔installation linkage beyond Geneva to at least two or three further cantons.
The deliverable is a reproducible framework for linking notifiable disease surveillance to
building infrastructure registries — reusable well past this project, and the kind of
infrastructure contribution that establishes a PI.

**WP2 — The dose–response between water temperature regime and legionellosis risk.**
Your comparative advantage. Rather than another association model, use the quasi-experimental
variation that exists: the 2022–23 energy-crisis setpoint reductions, staggered renovation and
heat-pump retrofits, regulatory changes. Difference-in-differences and event-study designs on
staggered adoption, with the identification assumptions stated and tested. **This is the section
that distinguishes you from an epidemiology group and from an engineering group** — it is
econometrics doing something neither would do, and it should be written to make that visible.

**WP3 — Mechanistic exposure layer.**
Couple building water-system physics (temperature profile, stagnation, recirculation, volume)
to an exposure model, so the risk estimates are mechanistically interpretable rather than purely
statistical. This is where a collaboration with a building-science group belongs — Empa or the
EPFL building-physics groups — and where a research stay does real work rather than decorating
the mobility statement.

**WP4 — Decision tool and joint health–energy evaluation.**
Optimal setpoint and intervention policy under joint objectives: cases averted, QALYs, energy
consumption, CO₂, cost. ICER and a Swiss-context cost-effectiveness analysis. Deliverable: an
open, documented tool for cantonal authorities and operators. Your quantitative finance
background — stress testing, scenario analysis, tail risk — is directly and honestly applicable
here, and tail behaviour genuinely matters for outbreak risk.

**Structure it so no work package is hostage to another.** WP2 must not require WP1 to have
succeeded in every canton; design it to run on Geneva alone if national extension stalls, and say
so explicitly. That single sentence pre-empts the most common feasibility objection.

### Independence — build it in deliberately

- Frame the cantonal study as **preliminary work you led**, in section 2 of the research plan.
  It is exactly what section 2 is for.
- The Ambizione must be **different in scope (national), in design (quasi-experimental, not
  retrospective-associational), and in discipline mix (building physics and health economics
  added)** — not just longer.
- **Have Keiser as a named collaborator or supporter, not the project leader.** A supporting
  letter from her that explicitly describes the line as yours is worth more than her absence.
- Consider whether a **different host unit** at UNIGE — or a co-affiliation — would make the
  independence claim easier to make. A referee reading "same institute, same PI, same corridor"
  supplies their own interpretation.
- Get the **detailed confirmation letter** to state in concrete terms that you will lead your own
  group and supervise your own doctoral students.

### Mobility — your weakest point, and the one to work on first

MSc, DEA, PhD and current post all at UNIGE, and staying. Mobility is an explicit assessment
criterion with its own evaluated statement. Options, strongest first:

1. **Move.** Unisanté (Lausanne), ISPM Bern, Swiss TPH (Basel) or EPFL would fix mobility *and*
   independence in a single decision. You have said you intend to stay, so I am not arguing for
   this — but it is the option that solves both problems at once, and it should be a considered
   decision rather than a default.
2. **Move within UNIGE** to a different institute, or take a formal co-affiliation, so the
   application is not "same PI, same institute, same everything".
3. **Substantial planned outgoing mobility during the grant** — concrete, named, agreed in
   advance, appearing in both the mobility statement and WP3. The Swiss National Reference Centre
   for Legionella, the SwissLEGIO network, Empa/EPFL building science, RIVM in the Netherlands
   (strong on Legionella and water systems), ECDC ELDSNet. A three-month stay with a named host
   and an agreed role, referenced in a work package, is worth far more than a list of conferences.

Whichever route: this needs deciding in **August**, because option 3 requires other people to
agree to host you and their agreement has to be in the dossier.

### Budget reality — face this now

The 2026 ceiling is roughly **CHF 250,000 of project funds over four years** (down from 400,000).
At SNSF doctoral rates plus employer social charges, **one PhD student for four years consumes
essentially the entire envelope.** There is no version of this where you fund a PhD student, a
postdoc, travel and computing.

Realistic options:
- One doctoral student for ~3.5 years, plus travel, computing, data and open access; you do WP2
  yourself. Probably the right shape — WP2 is where your distinctive contribution lies, so doing
  it yourself is also the correct scientific choice.
- One doctoral student for four years, with travel and research stays funded through other
  channels (institutional, mobility supplements, cantonal partners).
- Bring the SIG/OCEN/cantonal funding in as **declared co-funding**. This strengthens the
  application twice over: it shows resources beyond the SNSF envelope, and it is evidence that
  you already attract funding independently.

Confirm current SNSF doctoral salary rates and UNIGE employer costs with the grants office before
committing to a staffing plan — this arithmetic drives the scope, not the other way round.

Question 5 is the one I would resolve first. Multi-canton case data access is the load-bearing
assumption of the whole design, and it depends on people outside your control. If it is not
achievable, better to know in August than in October — the fallback is to go deeper on Geneva
(longer time series, mechanistic modelling, prospective environmental sampling) rather than
broader, and that is a different proposal.
