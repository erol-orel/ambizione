# Research plan — structure and page budget

`[VERIFY]` Assumed limit: **15 pages / 60,000 characters**, bibliography excluded. Single PDF,
no annexes, min. 10 pt, 1.5 line spacing. Confirm in the Call 2026 document before drafting to
length — if the real limit differs, the budget below scales proportionally.

## Page budget

| § | Section | Pages | Function in the reviewer's mind |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Summary | 0.5 | "Do I understand what this person will do, and do I want to keep reading?" |
| 1 | Current state of research in the field | 2.0–2.5 | "Does this applicant command the field?" |
| 2 | Current state of my own research | 1.0–1.5 | "Has this person earned the right to attempt this?" |
| 3 | Objectives and hypotheses | 1.0 | "What exactly is being claimed?" |
| 4 | Detailed research plan — work packages, methods, timeline, risks | 7.0–8.0 | "Will it work?" |
| 5 | Relevance, impact, and scientific independence | 1.0 | "Does it matter, and does it make this person a PI?" |
| 6 | Feasibility, environment and resources | 0.5 | "Are the pieces in place?" |
| — | Bibliography | excluded | — |

## What each section must do

### 0. Summary
Written last. Four moves in half a page: the problem, why it is unsolved, your approach, what
will exist at the end. No jargon in the first two sentences — a division panel contains people
outside your subfield and they will decide whether to read on.

### 1. Current state of research in the field
Not a literature review. An **argument** that ends in a gap. Structure it so that by the last
paragraph the reader has independently concluded that someone should do your project. Name the
competing groups and approaches honestly — a state of the art that omits your rivals reads as
either ignorance or evasion, and referees are frequently drawn from exactly those groups.

### 2. Current state of my own research
The bridge between section 1 and section 4, and the place where **independence** is established.
Show that your prior work produced the specific tool, insight, dataset or method that makes the
project possible now. Be explicit about your own contribution in collaborative work. If the
project departs from your supervisor's programme, say so here in plain terms rather than hoping
it is inferred.

### 3. Objectives and hypotheses
Three to four objectives, each stated as a falsifiable claim or a concrete deliverable, each
mapping onto a work package in section 4. Avoid objectives that cannot fail — "characterise",
"explore", "investigate" — unless the characterisation itself is the hard, valuable thing, in
which case say what makes it hard.

### 4. Detailed research plan
The bulk, and where feasibility is won or lost. Per work package:
- the question it answers and which objective it serves
- methods, in enough detail that a referee in your field can judge them — this is also where
  your **expertise is assessed**, so method choices should read as informed rather than default
- who does the work (you, PhD student, postdoc) and when
- what the expected outcome is, and **what you do if the result is negative or the method fails**
- dependencies on other work packages

Include a **Gantt chart or timeline figure** and explicit **milestones**. Do not build a plan in
which WP3 cannot begin until WP1 fully succeeds — serial dependency on an uncertain result is
the classic feasibility objection, and a reviewer who sees it will raise it.

Address risk in a dedicated subsection: the two or three things most likely to go wrong, and the
mitigation for each. Applicants routinely omit this out of fear it signals weakness. It signals
the opposite — an applicant who has not identified their own risks looks naive, and the panel
will identify them anyway.

### 5. Relevance, impact and scientific independence
Why the results matter — to the field first, then more broadly if there is an honest case. Then,
explicitly: how this grant establishes you as an independent researcher with your own line of
enquiry and your own group. Ambizione is a career instrument. A proposal that is scientifically
excellent but shows no trajectory towards independence is a mismatch with the scheme, and panels
are instructed to notice.

### 6. Feasibility, environment and resources
Why this host institution — infrastructure, collaborators, cohorts, archives, facilities.
Consistent with the statement of mobility and with the host institution's confirmation letter.
Any secured access (ethics approvals, data agreements, beam time, cohort access) belongs here,
because "secured" beats "will be sought".

## Drafting rules

- Write sections 2, 3 and 4 first. Section 1 is easiest to write and hardest to write *well*;
  writing it after the plan keeps it aimed at the gap rather than sprawling.
- One idea per paragraph, first sentence carries it. A referee reading 12 proposals in a weekend
  reads first sentences properly and the rest quickly.
- Figures earn their space. A concept figure in section 3 and a timeline in section 4 are worth
  more than the text they displace.
- Every claim about your own capability should be traceable to something in the CV or output
  list. Assertion without evidence is the weakest sentence type in a grant application.
