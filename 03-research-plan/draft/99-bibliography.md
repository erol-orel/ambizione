# Bibliography

> ## Warning — read before using this file
>
> **This is a skeleton of *topics*, not a reference list.** I have deliberately not generated
> formatted citations, because fabricated or subtly wrong references are the most damaging
> failure mode available in a grant application: wrong year, wrong journal, right author with the
> wrong paper, or a plausible article that does not exist. A referee drawn from this literature
> will notice, and one bad citation calls the applicant's judgement into question in a way a weak
> paragraph does not.
>
> The same warning applies with more force to `GESICA_Scientific_Base.md` and `ROADMAP.md` in the
> LiteRev-Evidence repository — those are automatically generated and contain specific
> performance figures attributed to named papers. **Verify every one against the actual article
> before any of it reaches this plan.**
>
> LiteRev is the right instrument for building this list. Use it, then check what it returns.

## Topics to populate

**§1.1 Surveillance and forecasting.** Serfling seasonal regression; Farrington and Farrington
Flexible outbreak detection; seasonal ARIMA and decomposition approaches; collaborative forecast
hubs and ensemble superiority; prehospital call data as a leading syndromic indicator.

**§1.2 Cold start.** Retrospective evaluations of early-pandemic forecast skill; forecasting
under short series; transfer learning and multi-region pooling in epidemic forecasting;
mechanistic models under parameter uncertainty.

**§1.3 Evidence as prior information.** Bayesian evidence synthesis; power priors; commensurate
priors; meta-analytic-predictive priors; structured expert elicitation; living systematic reviews;
LLM-based data extraction and its evaluation; risk-of-bias assessment.

**§1.3 Transportability.** Formal transportability and external validity; effect-modifier
distributions; generalisability of prediction models across settings.

**§1.4 Regime switching and extremes.** Hamilton's Markov-switching framework; hidden Markov
models in epidemiological surveillance; peaks-over-threshold and generalised Pareto estimation;
threshold selection diagnostics; extreme value methods in heat–health studies.

**§1.5 Evaluation.** Proper scoring rules (CRPS, logarithmic score); calibration and sharpness;
decision-curve analysis and net benefit; value-of-information analysis; the implementation gap
for clinical prediction models.

**§4 Domain.** Weather and EMS demand; heat and emergency presentations; out-of-hospital cardiac
arrest and temperature; ICU surge capacity; legionellosis epidemiology and building water systems;
Swiss surveillance infrastructure.
