## 2.2 Current state of personal research and competences required for the project

I studied quantitative economics and econometrics (MSc and DEA, University of Geneva), a discipline at the junction of statistics, mathematics and machine learning, built around modelling and prediction. I then worked for eleven years in quantitative finance on risk, extreme values, regime models and stress testing, and became a certified Financial Risk Manager (FRM), before completing a doctorate in biomedical sciences at Geneva (defended 18 December 2023). This combination provides the methods for forecasting rare events and the public-health training to apply them to emergency services.

### 2.2.1 Prediction under sparse and imperfect information

In [Orel 2022] I used socio-behavioural attributes to predict HIV status at the individual level for East and Southern Africa and determined the contexts where model relationships transferred. [Merzouki 2021] and [Esra 2023] performed similar studies. As last author of [Ng'ambi 2026] (accepted), I directed the classification of self-reported cardiovascular disease across harmonised WHO STEPS surveys, a senior role on the question of when model relationships transfer across settings.

### 2.2.2 Automated evidence synthesis

I conceived and built LiteRev [Orel 2023] with Aziza Merzouki (PhD, computer science), in Prof. Olivia Keiser's group. The tool automatically performs systematic searches using natural language or Boolean queries, removes duplicates and maps the literature. On my own initiative I raised CHF 30,000 from UNIGE, CHF 10,000 from the Fondation Ernst et Lucie Schmidheiny, CHF 10,000 from Venture Kick and CHF 20,000 from Mimosa. The systematic review of AI in emergency medical services [Edjinedja 2026] uses LiteRev in its methods as part of GESICA.

### 2.2.3 Outbreak and health-system modelling in Switzerland

In [Orel 2024] I compared clinical severity between Delta and Omicron sub-lineages in a Swiss tertiary centre. [Estill 2020] developed age-structured scenarios for the Swiss SARS-CoV-2 epidemic to support decisions under time pressure. That work helped motivate the present question. I also contributed to WHO African Region reporting and seroprevalence estimation [Nwosu 2021].

### 2.2.4 The prototype and test bed, LiteRev-Evidence

Since 2024, I have extended LiteRev into LiteRev-Evidence, a prototype for structured numerical extraction and modelling. In its current form it searches and deduplicates the literature, records screening decisions with PRISMA accounting, extracts study details and PICO elements (population, intervention, comparator and outcome), tracks the source and certainty of each extracted number, and pools results into distributions of plausible parameter values. It suggests explanatory variables with their supporting studies, identifies candidate outcomes, fits models across algorithm families with time-series validation, and builds compartmental model components from published parameter ranges. Evidence updates are reviewed before they are applied. These functions exist as a prototype only. None has yet been verified, measured or validated, and that is the work of this grant.

For GESICA I built the data foundation of the Geneva–Vaud–Neuchâtel network for influenza, COVID-19 and RSV, whose Geneva component HEADSTART uses. It includes an inventory of 28 surveillance sources (23 infectious and 5 environmental), each described by coverage, resolution, latency, access and quality. Within GeoAI4EI (Horizon Europe, 2026–2030) I contribute to the European epidemic-intelligence toolbox, which connects this work to European partners.

The prototype reduces development risk, since its literature-search, extraction and model-selection components already run in preliminary form. The grant focuses on the scientific questions that remain open, namely whether the extracted evidence is sufficiently reliable, whether it transfers to local settings and whether it improves forecasts under the registered comparison.

### 2.2.5 Linked data on a contrasting crisis type

I lead the data work for a cantonal study already under way (BASEC 2026-00324, ethics granted) that links confirmed legionellosis cases in Geneva to individual domestic hot-water installations. These linked data are the basis of the third test, on legionellosis case incidence, in year four.

My finance work provides experience in Bayesian hierarchical modelling, regime changes, extreme values and stress testing under misspecification. LiteRev-Evidence provides extraction and pooling. My doctorate, the Swiss COVID-19 work and GESICA provide the Geneva and public-health context.
