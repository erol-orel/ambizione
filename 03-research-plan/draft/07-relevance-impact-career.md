## 2.5 Relevance and impact

If published evidence does not improve forecasts, that is still a useful result. It shows where using published estimates as model inputs adds little or carries too much risk, and spares modellers and evidence platforms an untested assumption.

Results are published in open-access journals, with preprints where appropriate. The extraction benchmark, the forecasting benchmark, the library of evidence-derived inputs and the code are released as versioned, citable resources, and the findings go back to the participating services. The results and tools are also shared with GESICA and GeoAI4EI, which can carry them to the Franco-Swiss and European epidemic-intelligence networks.

The project will show whether outside evidence improves forecasts in the first weeks of a crisis, under which conditions, and with how much certainty. For the crisis types that pass validation and are authorised, it delivers a registered forecasting configuration and a dashboard in observation mode. Wider deployment to other crises is a possible next step after the grant, not a promised outcome.

I conceived and built LiteRev with Aziza Merzouki and Prof. Olivia Keiser. My current extension, LiteRev-Evidence, adds structured numerical extraction and modelling. It can turn published estimates into parameter distributions, but whether using those distributions improves forecasts in a new setting has not been established. My quantitative-finance work provides methods for rare events, regime changes and stress testing, and LiteRev-Evidence provides the evidence pipeline. The research question and the confirmatory analyses of HEADSTART are mine.
