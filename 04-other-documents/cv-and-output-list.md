# CV, achievement narratives and research output list

`[HIGH]` The SNSF uses a **narrative CV format**, not a classic academic CV. This is a genuine
change in what is being asked for: instead of listing everything, you select a small number of
achievements and explain their significance and your specific contribution.

`[VERIFY]` Working limits — confirm in the Call 2026 document:
- **Max 10 works in total** across all achievements, distributed as you choose.
- **Max ~4,350 characters (~1 A4 page)** per achievement narrative.

## How to use the format well

The constraint is the point. Ten works with a compelling story beat forty works listed flat, and
the panel knows the format is designed to reward judgement about your own work.

- **Distribute deliberately.** Concentrating several works under one achievement signals a
  sustained line of research; spreading them signals breadth. Choose which claim you want to
  make — do not distribute by accident.
- **Say what *you* did.** In collaborative work, name your contribution explicitly. Reviewers
  cannot infer it from author position across fields with different conventions.
- **Explain significance, do not assert it.** "Highly cited" is weak; "this is now the standard
  method in X, used by groups A, B and C" is evidence.
- **Non-publication outputs count** — software, datasets, instruments, protocols, clinical
  guidelines, policy contributions. In fields where these are the real contribution, using the
  quota on them is the right call, not a fallback.
- **Point forward.** Each narrative should quietly support the claim that you can do the proposed
  project. The CV and the research plan are read together.

## Handling weaknesses

If there is a thin period, a field change, or a slow start, address it factually in the CV
rather than leaving a silence for the reviewer to fill. Documented interruptions (parental leave,
illness, care duties, service) should be stated — they are taken into account, and the SNSF
assesses output relative to academic age, not calendar age.
