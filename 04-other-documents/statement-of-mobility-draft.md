### Past mobility and scientific trajectory

I completed my academic training at the University of Geneva and have conducted my research
there. I state this at the outset rather than leave it to be inferred: my geographic mobility has
been limited, and the case I make below rests on other grounds.

What it contains instead is a change of field more radical than most changes of address. After a
master's in quantitative economics and a postgraduate degree in econometrics (DEA, 2006,
supervised by Prof. Eva Cantoni), I spent fifteen years outside academia as a quantitative analyst
and risk manager, estimating rare and costly events: extreme value methods for non-Gaussian tails,
regime and factor models, stress testing and scenario analysis. In 2019 I left that career for a
doctorate in biomedical sciences, defended in December 2023.

That discontinuity is the source of what I bring to public health. The methods at the centre of
this project — regime-switching representations of latent system states, extreme value estimation
for rare transitions, decision-analytic evaluation of forecasts — are not ones I acquired in any
research group I have belonged to. I brought them from another domain, and my contribution has
been to carry them across that boundary into problems where the structure is identical and the
apparatus largely absent.

I have also moved between scientific communities and worked internationally: doctoral work on HIV
prediction in East and Southern Africa `[[with collaborations in Malawi, South Africa, Cameroon —
adjust to your actual involvement]]`; pandemic-period contributions to seroprevalence estimation
in Cameroon (*Nature Communications*, 2021), to an international collaboration supporting the
sub-Saharan African COVID-19 response, and to WHO African Region reporting; and since 2024 the
cross-border **GESICA** consortium with HUG emergency medicine, the Université Marie et Louis
Pasteur and HES-SO, alongside a Horizon Europe consortium on epidemic intelligence. My scientific
networks are not local, even where my address has been.

### Why the University of Geneva, and why this institute

Three reasons specific to this project rather than to the institution's general standing.

**The data exist here and nowhere else.** The project requires operational health-system series —
dispatch, emergency presentations, intensive care occupancy — linked to surveillance and
environmental covariates. These are reachable through partnerships built over several years with
HUG emergency medicine and the Geneva emergency response system through GESICA, and evidenced by
joint publication. For the waterborne validation domain I hold a linkage between the cantonal
legionellosis registry and individual water installations, under ethics approval already granted
(BASEC 2026-00324), with no equivalent I am aware of elsewhere.

**The methodological environment matches the work.** The host institute holds the
infectious-disease and evidence-extraction expertise the project consumes, and the collaboration
with Data Science for Digital Health supplies the biomedical natural language processing and
information retrieval that WP1's extraction work requires.

**I have not manufactured a move I did not make.** I will conduct this project in the institute
where I already work. Relocating to another department to make the word "independence" look
cleaner would have cost the project its operational base — the emergency-system partnerships, the
cantonal linkage under BASEC 2026-00324 — and changed nothing real, since the units share a
campus. My independence rests on facts a reviewer can check rather than on an address: **100% of
my research time on a programme I lead as principal investigator**, a scientific question and
confirmatory design that belong to no existing programme, my own ethics approvals, and a host
confirmation signed at institute level by the Director rather than by any group leader whose
grants currently fund me. The mobility I claim is the outgoing stay below, and I claim nothing
beyond it.
