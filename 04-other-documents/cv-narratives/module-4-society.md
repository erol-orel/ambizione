# Module 4 — Impact beyond the scientific community, for broader society

> Outputs cited here: **2** — COVID-19 epidemiological dashboard for the WHO African Region
> (research resource); Legionellosis in Geneva: linked case–installation study, BASEC 2026-00324
> (protocol and dataset, with secured cantonal funding).

---

**Evidence for cantonal public health, with funding I raised.** Since 2025 I have led the data
work on a study linking confirmed legionellosis cases in Geneva to individual domestic hot water
installations, with technical, meteorological and territorial covariates. The question is
practical and current: Switzerland is lowering hot water temperatures to meet energy targets,
*Legionella* proliferation is temperature-dependent, and the trade-off has not been quantified
with linked real-world data. Building it required geocoding cases, matching them to installation
records, and constructing a linkage that to my knowledge exists nowhere else. Ethics approval was
granted (BASEC 2026-00324), and the results and prioritisation tool go to the cantonal doctor to
support surveillance and prevention planning.

What I would highlight is not the analysis but the assembly. The study is funded by three
partners I approached and convinced — **Services Industriels de Genève, the Office cantonal de
l'énergie, and the Service du Médecin Cantonal** — spanning a utility, an energy regulator and a
public health authority, each with different reasons to care and different constraints. Bringing
them to a common protocol, and satisfying the data-protection requirements of all three, was most
of the work. `[[Confirm the total figure — approximately CHF 90,000.]]`

**Emergency preparedness with the people who respond.** Through GESICA I work directly with HUG
emergency medicine and the Geneva emergency response system on decision support for health
crises. The orientation of that work, and of the project I am proposing, is towards the users
rather than the literature: the escalation thresholds that matter are the ones at which an
emergency service can actually do something different, and those can only be established by
asking the people who would do it.

**Pandemic response.** During COVID-19 I built and maintained an epidemiological dashboard for
the WHO African Region, used for regional situational awareness, and contributed to modelling
that informed Swiss federal and cantonal planning. This was work whose value was measured in
whether it was usable in the week it was needed, not in citations, and it durably changed what I
consider a successful methodological contribution.

**Teaching practitioners.** I lecture in statistics and epidemiology on the MAS in Public Health,
a programme largely attended by practising clinicians and public health professionals. The
transfer here is direct: methods reaching people who apply them in cantonal and hospital settings.

**Why this matters for the work I want to do next.** A recurring theme across all of the above is
that technically sound analysis frequently fails to change anything, because it was never
designed to. The project I am proposing takes that failure as its object: it evaluates crisis
forecasts on whether they alter decisions and outcomes rather than on statistical accuracy, and
it treats the elicitation of decision thresholds from emergency responders as a research
contribution rather than an implementation detail.
