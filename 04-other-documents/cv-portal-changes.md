### Achievement 1 (replace). Prediction across settings when data are sparse

During my doctorate I used machine learning on large population surveys to predict individual
HIV status from social and behavioural characteristics across East and Southern Africa, and I
examined where a model learnt in one country held in another [1]. Related work in the group
addressed treatment interruption in South Africa [2] and country clusters by behaviour [3].
During the pandemic I applied the same skills to Swiss and African data, comparing the clinical
severity of Delta and Omicron in a Swiss university hospital [4], contributing to the Swiss
age-structured scenario model used for planning under time pressure [5], and mentoring the
analysis of a seroprevalence study in Cameroon as senior statistician [6]. As last author I
supervised the statistical analysis and the code of a machine-learning classification of
cardiovascular disease across WHO STEPS surveys, accepted in BMC Global and Public Health [7].
These studies gave me direct experience of when a model transfers between settings, the question
at the centre of my Ambizione project.

Outputs: [1] Orel 2022 PLoS ONE; [2] Esra 2023 JAIDS; [3] Merzouki 2021 PeerJ; [4] Orel 2024
CMI Communications; [5] Estill 2020 F1000Research 9:646; [6] Nwosu 2021 Nature Communications;
[7] Ng'ambi 2026 BMC Global and Public Health, accepted, preprint doi:10.64898/2026.02.23.26346870.

### Achievement 2 (replace). LiteRev and LiteRev-Evidence, tools that read the literature

I conceived and built LiteRev with Aziza Merzouki and Olivia Keiser, a platform that searches
the open literature from plain-language or Boolean queries, removes duplicates, maps the
retrieved studies into topics and supports double screening with PRISMA accounting. In its
published evaluation it saved 56% of the screening work compared with a manual review (work saved
over sampling) [1]. It grew
out of a semi-automated review of HIV in Malawi [2]. The tool was funded by CHF 70,000 obtained
with the group (UNIGE, Fondation Ernst et Lucie Schmidheiny, Venture Kick, Mimosa) and is used by
other teams, including a systematic review of artificial intelligence in emergency medical
services within the Franco-Swiss GESICA consortium, which I co-authored [3]. Since 2024 I have
extended it into LiteRev-Evidence, a prototype that extracts numbers from studies with their
uncertainty, pools them into parameter distributions and fits first forecasting models. These
parts exist as a prototype and have not yet been validated as one pipeline. Testing whether such
extracted evidence can improve forecasts at the start of a health crisis is the aim of my
Ambizione project.

Outputs: [1] Orel 2023 JMIR; [2] Thiabaud 2020 JMIR; [3] Edjinedja 2026, submitted to Machine
Learning and Knowledge Extraction.

### Achievement 3 (new). Data for public health decisions in Geneva, and teaching

Since 2024 I have worked on Geneva's surveillance and emergency data. Within GESICA I mapped the
variables and data sources for influenza, COVID-19, RSV and heat waves, in an inventory of 28
sources documented for coverage, resolution, latency, access and quality, and I contribute to the
European epidemic-intelligence toolbox of GeoAI4EI (Horizon Europe). I wrote the statistical
analysis plan of a cantonal study that links confirmed legionellosis cases to the hot-water
installations of buildings (CHF 30,000 each from Services Industriels de Genève and the Office
cantonal de l'énergie, a further CHF 30,000 requested from the médecin cantonal), and I carry out
its full analysis under ethics approval BASEC 2026-00324. During the pandemic I helped build the
COVID-19 epidemiological dashboard of the WHO African Region. Since 2024 I lecture in statistics
and epidemiology on the MAS in Public Health, I have supervised eight MAS theses and mentored about
six junior researchers at the Institute, and I review occasionally for journals.
