# Self-assessment — read your application as the panel will

Run this before submission, on the finished dossier, in one sitting. Score honestly; a 3 you
record now is a 3 you can still fix.

## Scoring lens

The official criteria are **scientific relevance, topicality, originality, feasibility, and
suitability of methods**, applied to the project — plus the applicant's qualifications, which
are judged partly *from the research plan itself*, plus **mobility**.

Score each 1 (poor) to 5 (excellent). Anything below 4 needs a specific fix, written down.

## The three questions that decide it

Ambizione applications are rarely rejected for being uninteresting. They are rejected for these:

**Is this person becoming an independent researcher, or continuing someone else's programme?**
If a reviewer cannot answer this from the dossier, the answer they assume is the unfavourable one.

**Will this actually work in four years with these resources?**
Ambition that outruns feasibility is the most common fatal flaw, and the reduced budget ceiling
makes it more common in this call than in previous ones.

**Why here, why now, why this person?**
The three must be answered together. Any one of them left implicit weakens the other two.

## The tie-break

Where applications are of equal quality and no further differentiation is possible, the SNSF
decides **by lot**. Landing in that band means surrendering the outcome to chance. Everything in
this checklist is aimed at being clearly above it rather than comfortably inside it.
